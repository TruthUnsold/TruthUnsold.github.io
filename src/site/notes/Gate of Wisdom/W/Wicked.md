# Define

- **Hebrew:** rasha (רָשָׁע) — "wicked, guilty, in the wrong." 
	- Its root, rasha (רָשַׁע), is a courtroom term. In Deuteronomy 25:1 the judges "justify the righteous (tsaddiq, צַדִּיק) and condemn the wicked (rasha)." 
	- The rasha is the one found guilty and out of right standing with God and the community, and the word describes a verdict on conduct as much as inner character. 
- **Greek (LXX):** asebes (ἀσεβής) — "ungodly, irreverent." 
	- The Greek leans toward lack of reverence for God, while the Hebrew leans toward guilt and standing before the Judge.

# Related Words

- tsaddiq (צַדִּיק) — [[Gate of Wisdom/Right Standing/Righteous\|Righteous]], the direct opposite of rasha (Genesis 18:23-25; Proverbs 10:3, 10:7)
- resha (רֶשַׁע) — wickedness, the noun form (Deuteronomy 9:4-5)
- ra (רַע) — [[Gate of Wisdom/B/evil\|evil]], used of Sodom's men in Genesis 13:13
- chatta (חַטָּא) — sinner, paired with ra in Genesis 13:13
- chata (חָטָא) — to [[Gate of Wisdom/S/Sin\|Sin]], miss the mark (Genesis 18:20)
- avon (עָוֹן) — iniquity (Genesis 19:15)
- chamas (חָמָס) — violence (Genesis 6:11)
- saphah (סָפָה) — to sweep away or consume, the verb in "Will you consume" (Genesis 18:23; 19:15, 19:17)

# Additional Information

- The question in Genesis 18:23 is the first time rasha appears in Scripture. Abraham uses it when he approaches YHWH to plead for Sodom.
- Abraham repeats the pairing in 18:25: "Be it far from you to do things like this, to kill the righteous with the wicked, so that the righteous should be as the wicked."
- Abraham's question rests on the idea that the Judge of all the earth will do mishpat (מִשְׁפָּט, "justice" [[Gate of Wisdom/Right Standing/Judgments (Mishpatim)\|Judgments (Mishpatim)]]), which echoes 18:19 (Genesis 18:25).
- The label for Sodom was already set in Genesis 13:13: "Now the men of Sodom were exceedingly wicked and sinners against Yahweh."
- The wicked are not condemned arbitrarily. Ezekiel 18:23 says YHWH takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but in their turning from their way and living. See also Ezekiel 33:11.

- Deuteronomy 25:1 — judges justify the righteous and condemn the wicked
- Psalm 1:5-6 — the wicked will not stand in the judgment; YHWH knows the way of the righteous
- Proverbs 17:15 — justifying the wicked and condemning the righteous are both an abomination to YHWH

- Ezekiel 18:23, 18:32; 33:11 — YHWH does not delight in the death of the wicked
- Genesis 18:19 — "righteousness and justice" are the opposite of the conduct Sodom is judged for
