Two different words: eved (male) and amah (female). 

# Male Servant
# Define

- Hebrew: eved (עֶבֶד) — servant, slave, bondservant; one who works for and belongs to a household under its head. Hebrew mindset: an eved is a member of the household with a place in its covenant life, not only a laborer. The word is also an honorable title for those who serve Yahweh (Exodus 14:31; Numbers 12:7; Isaiah 42:1).
- Greek (LXX): pais (παῖς) — "boy, child, servant." The LXX of Exodus 20:10 reads "your pais." Often rendered eved as doulos (δοῦλος), as in Exodus 21:2.

# Related Words

- [[Gate of Wisdom/A/Avad\|avad]] (עָבַד) — to work, serve, labor. This is the verb in Exodus 20:9, "Six days you shall labor." The servant (eved) is "one who avad-s," and the Sabbath rests both.
- avodah (עֲבֹדָה) — work, service — Exodus 20:9
- beit avadim (בֵּית עֲבָדִים) — "house of slaves/bondage" — Exodus 20:2
- sachir (שָׂכִיר) — hired worker — Leviticus 25:40
- doulos (δοῦλος) — Greek slave/servant — Philippians 2:7
- diakonos (διάκονος) — Greek attendant, minister — John 12:26

# Additional Information

- The same root appears in the opening of the Ten Words, where Yahweh brought them out of the "house of bondage" (Exodus 20:2). In Deuteronomy's version, Israel's own time as an eved in Egypt is the reason servants rest (Deuteronomy 5:14-15).
- The Hebrew eved was released in the seventh year (Exodus 21:2). The pattern of seven carries through servitude and rest alike.

# Connections

- Exodus 23:12 — rest "so that your servant may be refreshed"
- Leviticus 25:39-43 — a fellow Israelite is not to be treated as a slave, because God brought them out of Egypt
- Deuteronomy 5:14-15 — Sabbath tied to remembering slavery in Egypt
- Isaiah 42:1; 49:6 — the Servant of Yahweh
- Philippians 2:7 — Messiah took the form of a doulos
- John 8:34-36 — the slave and the son in the house

# Female Servant

# Define

- Hebrew: amah (אָמָה) — female servant, maidservant, handmaid. Hebrew mindset: an amah is a woman of the household under the head's authority, who could also become a wife or concubine (Exodus 21:7-11). The word is also used humbly by women of themselves before God or men (1 Samuel 1:11; Ruth 3:9).
- Greek (LXX): paidiskē (παιδίσκη) — "young female servant, maidservant." The LXX of Exodus 20:10 reads "your paidiskē." In the NT, Galatians 4:22-23 uses paidiskē for Hagar.

# Related Words

- shifchah (שִׁפְחָה) — maidservant, a near synonym — Genesis 16:1; 1 Samuel 25:41
- eved (עֶבֶד) — the male counterpart — Exodus 20:10
- doule (δούλη) — Greek female slave/servant, as in "the doule of the Lord" — Luke 1:38
- ger (גֵּר) — sojourner, also named in the verse — Exodus 20:10

# Additional Information

- The amah is named separately from the eved so that no member of the household is overlooked by the Sabbath command (Exodus 20:10).
- Exodus 21:7-11 sets protections for a daughter sold as an amah: she is not to go free as the men do, but she may not be treated treacherously or deprived of food, clothing, and marital rights.
- Sarah called Hagar both shifchah (Genesis 16:1) and amah (Genesis 21:10), so the two words overlap.
- Exodus 23:12 names "the son of your handmaid" among those refreshed by the Sabbath.

# Connections

- Exodus 21:7-11 — laws for the amah
- Exodus 23:12 — rest for the handmaid's son
- Deuteronomy 5:14 — the parallel Sabbath command
- 1 Samuel 1:11 — Hannah calls herself Yahweh's amah
- Ruth 3:9 — Ruth calls herself Boaz's amah
- Luke 1:38 — Mary: "Behold, the servant of the Lord"
- Galatians 4:22-31 — Hagar as paidiskē, used allegorically
