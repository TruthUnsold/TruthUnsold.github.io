# Define

- Hebrew: tignov (תִּגְנֹב), from the verb ganav (גָּנַב), "to steal, take by stealth." 
	- Hebrew mindset: ganav carries the sense of secret, covert taking, the thief who works unseen (Job 24:14; Jeremiah 2:26). It covers property (Exodus 22:1-4), and it also covers the taking of a person. Kidnapping a man to sell or hold him is called ganav and carries the death penalty (Exodus 21:16; Deuteronomy 24:7). 
- Greek: kleypseis (κλέψεις), from klepto (κλέπτω), "to steal." 
	- Matthew 19:18; Romans 13:9; Ephesians 4:28

- pleonexia (πλεονεξία): Greek greed, grasping for more — Luke 12:15.

# Additional Information

- The Torah assigns restitution rather than death for property theft. A stolen ox is repaid fivefold and a sheep fourfold (Exodus 22:1), and if the thief cannot pay, he is sold for his theft (Exodus 22:3).
- If the stolen animal is found alive in his possession, he repays double (Exodus 22:4).
- A thief caught breaking in at night may be struck down without bloodguilt, but not in daylight (Exodus 22:2-3).
- A thief who confesses voluntarily repays the principal plus a fifth and brings a guilt offering (Leviticus 6:1-7).
- Leviticus 19:11 groups stealing with dealing falsely and lying to one another, which shows ganav as a breach of honesty and trust, not only of property.
- Withholding a hired worker's wages is treated as oppression, "The wages of a hired servant shall not remain with you all night until the morning" (Leviticus 19:13; Deuteronomy 24:14-15).
- Achan's theft of the devoted things at Jericho brought defeat on all Israel and his own death (Joshua 7:1, 11, 20-26).
- Taking a person is capital: "He who kidnaps a man, and sells him, or if he is found in his hand, shall surely be put to death" (Exodus 21:16).
- The prophets name stealing among Israel's sins (Hosea 4:2; Jeremiah 7:9), and Zechariah sees a flying scroll of curse against every thief (Zechariah 5:3-4).
- Yeshua names the thief as one who comes "only to steal, kill, and destroy" (John 10:10), and lists theft among the evils that come out of the heart (Matthew 15:19).
- Paul turns the command toward giving: "Let him who stole steal no more, but rather let him labor... that he may have something to give to him who has need" (Ephesians 4:28).
