# Define
- Hebrew: ḥaṭṭāʾt (חַטָּאת) sin, sin-offering; fundamentally means “missing the mark,” failing to meet God’s standard, going astray from the right path. It emphasizes the act of deviating from God’s will rather than deliberate rebellion.
	- Three major Hebrew sin-words. Psalm 51:1-2 asks for pesha to be blotted out, avon to be washed, and chatta'ah to be cleansed.
		- ḥaṭṭāʾt = “sin” – missing the mark
			- Missing the mark. The verb chata (חָטָא) is used of slingers who "could sling a stone at a hair and not miss" (Judges 20:16). Chatta'ah is the act (missing the mark), and it carries a stated penalty (Ezekiel 18:4; Romans 6:23).
			- The act itself, and the [[Gate of Wisdom/Consequence/Disobedience/Penalty\|Penalty]] attached to it: "The soul who sins (chata) shall die" (Ezekiel 18:4, 20; Numbers 15:27-28). The same word also names the sin offering (Leviticus 4:3).
			- Sacrifice reaches chatta'ah most directly, while pesha and avon rest on mercy.
		- ʿāwōn = “iniquity” – crookedness, guilt, perversion
			- Iniquity, meaning twisted or perverted conduct (Jeremiah 3:21), and also the guilt and punishment that result.
			- The [[Gate of Wisdom/P/Punishment\|Punishment]] carried by the person: "My punishment (avon) is greater than I can bear" (Genesis 4:13, WEB). It is "borne" (Leviticus 5:1; Numbers 14:34; Ezekiel 18:20). The punishment is not added afterward. It is part of the weight the person already carries
			- On [[Gate of Wisdom/Appointed Time/Yom HaKippurim\|Yom HaKippurim]] the goat carries the avonot, the pish'ei, and the chattotam (Leviticus 16:21).
			- The typical idiom is nasa avon (נָשָׂא עָוֹן), "to bear iniquity." The person carries the guilt and its consequence (Leviticus 5:1; 20:17, 19; Numbers 14:34; Ezekiel 4:4-6).
			- Avon is the punishment as carried, the guilt-weight that rests on the person (Genesis 4:13; Leviticus 5:1).
		- pesha = “[[Gate of Wisdom/T/Transgression\|Transgression]]/rebellion” – deliberate revolt
			- Transgression or rebellion, a breach against a party one is bound to. It is used of nations rebelling against a ruler (1 Kings 12:19; 2 Kings 8:22) and of Israel against YHWH (Isaiah 1:2).
			- The trigger for judgment: "For three transgressions... and for four, I will not turn away its punishment" (Amos 1:3, WEB; also Amos 3:14).
			- Pesha is the rebellious breach that calls judgment (Amos 1:3; Isaiah 1:2-5).
	- Pesha can be the root, chatta'ah the act, avon the result
		- Job 34:37 says the wicked person "adds rebellion (pesha) to his sin (chatta'ah)". Rebellion is the escalation of the act.
		- Psalm 107:17 links the two: fools are afflicted because of their pesha and their avonot, which shows the punishment (avon) growing out of the rebellion.
	- Forgiveness changes the outcome, but consequences may remain

- Greek: 
	- Most common LXX rendering of חַטָּאת: ἁμαρτία (hamartia) – “sin, failure to hit the target” (the primary NT word for sin; used over 170 times).  
	- Other occasional equivalents: ἁμάρτημα (hamartēma) – “sin, sinful act.”
