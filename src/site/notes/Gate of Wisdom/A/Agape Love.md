# Define

- **Greek**: agapē (ἀγάπη), love, with the verb agapaō (ἀγαπάω), to love. In 2 John it is the sphere of the blessing, "in truth and love" (2 John 1:3), and it is defined by the writer himself: "This is love, that we should walk according to his commandments" (2 John 1:6). In context it is love shown by action and obedience, not a feeling alone.
- **Hebrew**: ahavah (אַהֲבָה), love, with the verb ahav (אָהַב), to love. In the Hebrew mindset love is covenantal and shown in what one does, so it is joined to keeping commandments:
    - Deut 6:5 and 11:1: love YHWH, and keep His charge and commandments
    - Deut 7:9: He keeps covenant and loving kindness with "those who love him and keep his commandments"
    - Deut 10:12–13: love Him, walk in His ways, keep His commandments


# Related Words

- **chesed (חֶסֶד)**: covenant loyalty, steadfast love. Its LXX rendering eleos (ἔλεος) is the "mercy" of 2 John 1:3. Exod 34:6 joins chesed and emet.
- **emet (אֱמֶת) / alētheia (ἀλήθεια)**: truth, paired with love in 2 John 1:3.
- **shalom (שָׁלוֹם) / eirēnē (εἰρήνη)**: peace, in the same blessing (2 John 1:3).
- **mitzvah (מִצְוָה) / entolē (ἐντολή)**: commandment, the thing love is walked out through (2 John 1:5–6). [[Gate of Wisdom/Right Standing/Commands (Mitzvot)\|Commands (Mitzvot)]]
- **phileō (φιλέω)**: to love, to have affection. John uses it of the Father's love for the Son (John 5:20) and in Yeshua's questions to Peter (John 21:15–17).
- **rachamim (רַחֲמִים)**: compassion, tender mercy (Ps 103:13; Hos 11:4).
- **dod (דּוֹד)**: beloved, love between husband and wife (Song 1:2; 2:4).

# Additional Information

- **The word is defined by its context, not by itself.** The same word is used of wrong objects:
    - 2 Sam 13:15: Amnon's "hatred" for Tamar was greater than the "love" (ahavah, אַהֲבָה; LXX agapē) with which he had loved her.
    - John 3:19: people "loved the darkness" (ēgapēsan, ἠγάπησαν).
    - 2 Tim 4:10: Demas "loved this present world."
    - Scripture therefore does not treat agapē as a special word used only of God. John supplies its meaning through the commandment.
- John's treatment of love:
	- Love is the "old" commandment. 2 John 1:5 says it is not new but "from the beginning." It traces to Lev 19:18 and Deut 6:5, and Yeshua calls it "new" in John 13:34 (see also 1 John 2:7–8).
	- John writes "in truth and love" (2 John 1:3), and "in truth" with love (2 John 1:1). Love apart from the truth is not the love he describes here.
	- It's a deed. 1 John 3:18: "let's not love in word only, neither with the tongue only, but in deed and truth." 1 John 3:16: the model is Yeshua laying down His life.
	- Love is tied to keeping commandments in every place John defines it: John 14:15, 21, 23; 15:10; 1 John 2:5; 5:2–3; 2 John 1:6.
