# Define

- Hebrew: kabbed (כַּבֵּד), the command form of the verb kavad (כָּבַד). Its root meaning is "to be heavy, weighty," and in this form it means "give weight to, treat as weighty." 
	- Hebrew mindset: honor is something you do, not a feeling you have. To honor is to treat someone as heavy, carrying real weight in your life, the opposite of treating them as light (see qalal in Related Words). It includes respect, provision, and care (Genesis 45:9-11; 47:12; John 19:26-27). .
- Greek (LXX): tima (τίμα), the command form of timao (τιμάω), "to value, honor, show respect." 
	- Ephesians 6:2 and Matthew 15:4 quote the command with the same word.

# Related Words

- kavod (כָּבוֹד): weight, glory, honor. This is the noun from the same root, used for the glory of Yahweh (Exodus 16:10; 24:16). Parents are given weight in the way God is given weight (Proverbs 3:9).
- kaved (כָּבֵד): heavy, as in Moses' heavy hands (Exodus 17:12) and Pharaoh's heavy heart (Exodus 9:7).
- qalal (קָלַל): to treat as light, to curse. This is the opposite of kavad, and it is what the one who curses his parents does, with a death penalty attached (Exodus 21:17; Leviticus 20:9).
- qalah (קָלָה): to dishonor, make light of — Deuteronomy 27:16.
- yare (יָרֵא): to fear. Leviticus 19:3 says "Each one of you shall fear his mother and his father," which brings the fear studied in Exodus 20:20 into the command.
- hadar (הָדַר): to honor, show splendor — Leviticus 19:32.
- yaqar (יָקָר): precious, valued — Psalm 116:15.
- time (τιμή): Greek honor, value — 1 Timothy 5:17; 1 Peter 2:17.
- doxazo (δοξάζω): to glorify. The LXX usually renders kavod as doxa (δόξα), as in Exodus 16:10.

# Additional Information

- Both parents are named together. Proverbs 1:8 and 6:20 pair the father's instruction with the mother's teaching..
- Yeshua applied it to material support. He rebuked those who used a vow (Corban) to avoid providing for their parents, and he quoted both the command and its penalty (Matthew 15:3-6; Mark 7:9-13).
- Dishonor is serious in the Torah: striking or cursing a parent carried the death penalty (Exodus 21:15, 17), a rebellious son was brought before the elders (Deuteronomy 21:18-21), and a curse is placed on the one who dishonors his father or mother (Deuteronomy 27:16).
- The pattern of honor toward the aged extends beyond parents: "Rise up before the gray head, and honor the face of an old man" (Leviticus 19:32).
- Honor is returned by Yahweh to those who honor Him: "those who honor me I will honor" (1 Samuel 2:30).
