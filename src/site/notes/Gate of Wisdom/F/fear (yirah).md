# Define

- Hebrew: yir'ah (יִרְאָה), written here as yir'ato (יִרְאָתוֹ) — "His fear." It is the noun from the same root as yara (יָרֵא), meaning reverent awe, the fear of God that governs behavior. Hebrew mindset: the fear of God is not a feeling alone but a lived posture of obedience that keeps a person from sin, "that you won't sin" (Exodus 20:20; Deuteronomy 10:12; Ecclesiastes 12:13).
- Greek (LXX): phobos (φόβος) — "fear, awe, reverence." The LXX has "that the fear of Him may be in you" (Exodus 20:20). In the NT the phrase "fear of God/the Lord" uses the same word (Acts 9:31; 2 Corinthians 7:1).

# Related Words

- yir'at Yahweh (יִרְאַת יְהוָה) — fear of Yahweh — Proverbs 1:7; 9:10
- yare (יָרֵא) — one who fears God — Genesis 22:12; Psalm 112:1
- morah (מוֹרָא) — awe, reverence — Malachi 1:6; 2:5
- eulabeia (εὐλάβεια) — reverent caution — Hebrews 12:28
- eusebeia (εὐσέβεια) — godliness, devout reverence — 1 Timothy 4:7-8

# Additional Information

- The fear of God is the stated purpose of the testing: "that his fear may be before you, that you won't sin" (Exodus 20:20).
- The midwives acted from it and spared the boys (Exodus 1:17), and Abraham showed it by not withholding Isaac (Genesis 22:12).
- Abraham feared the lack of it in Gerar: "There is no fear of God in this place" (Genesis 20:11).
- Israel's fear moved from terror at the sea to reverence: "the people feared Yahweh" (Exodus 14:31).

# Connections

- Proverbs 1:7; 8:13; 9:10 — the beginning of knowledge and wisdom
