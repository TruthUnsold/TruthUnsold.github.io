# Define

- Hebrew: tira'u (תִּירָאוּ), from the verb yara (יָרֵא) — to fear, be afraid, be terrified. Here it is a prohibition (al tira'u, "do not fear") answering the people's trembling at the thunder, lightning, trumpet, and smoking mountain (Exodus 20:18-19). Hebrew mindset: the verb names a gut-level reaction to a threatening power. It is the same word used in Yahweh's repeated command "do not be afraid" (Genesis 15:1; Exodus 14:13; Isaiah 41:10).
- Greek (LXX): tharseite (θαρσεῖτε) — "take courage, be of good cheer." The LXX turns the prohibition into a positive command to be bold. The same word appears in Matthew 14:27, "Cheer up! It is I. Don't be afraid."

# Related Words

- chathath (חָתַת) — to be dismayed, shattered — Joshua 1:9
- arats (עָרַץ) — to be in dread, tremble — Deuteronomy 1:29; Joshua 1:9
- pachad (פַּחַד) — dread, terror — Exodus 15:16
- eymah (אֵימָה) — terror — Exodus 15:16
- deilia (δειλία) — cowardly fear — 2 Timothy 1:7

# Additional Information

- This is the fear of danger. The people feared the display of God's power and stood far off (Exodus 20:18-19).
- Moses commands them not to fear in this sense, because God came to test them (nasah), not to destroy them (Exodus 20:20).
- The same command is given at the sea, "Don't be afraid. Stand still" (Exodus 14:13), and by the angel to Hagar (Genesis 21:17).

# Connections

- Genesis 15:1; 26:24 — Yahweh's "do not fear" to Abram and Isaac
- Joshua 1:9 — do not be afraid or dismayed
- Isaiah 41:10; 43:1 — "do not fear, for I am with you"
- Matthew 14:27; 28:5, 10 — "do not be afraid"
- 1 John 4:18 — perfect love casts out the fear that has torment
