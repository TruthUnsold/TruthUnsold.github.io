# Define

- Hebrew: tirtzach (תִּרְצָח), from the verb ratsach (רָצַח), "to murder, slay." 
	- Hebrew mindset: ratsach is not the general word for killing. It is used for the unlawful taking of a human life, and in the Torah it is never used for killing in battle. 
	- It is the word for the "manslayer" (rotzeach, רֹצֵחַ) who flees to a city of refuge (Numbers 35:6, 11-12; Deuteronomy 4:41-43; 19:3-6). 
	- It can cover unintentional killing as well as intentional (Numbers 35:11; Deuteronomy 19:4), which is why the Torah separates the two cases (Exodus 21:12-14; Numbers 35:16-25). 
	- It is also used of the avenger of blood putting the murderer to death (Numbers 35:27, 30).
- Greek (LXX): phoneuseis (φονεύσεις), from phoneuo (φονεύω), "to murder." 
	- Matthew 5:21; 19:18; Romans 13:9; James 2:11.

# Related Words

- harag (הָרַג): to kill, slay. A broader word used for murder (Genesis 4:8; Psalm 94:6), for killing in war (Judges 9:54), and for judicial killing (Exodus 32:27).
- muth (מוּת), in the hiphil hemit (הֵמִית): to put to death, cause to die. This is the word in the penalty laws, "he shall surely be put to death" (Exodus 21:12, 15, 16).
- nakah (נָכָה): to strike, smite — Exodus 21:12, 18, 20.
- shafach dam (שָׁפַךְ דָּם): to shed blood — Genesis 9:6; Numbers 35:33.
- dam (דָּם): blood, bloodguilt — Deuteronomy 19:10; Hosea 4:2.
- rotzeach (רֹצֵחַ): murderer, manslayer — Numbers 35:16-21; Job 24:14.
- apokteino (ἀποκτείνω): Greek "to kill," the broader word, as in Matthew 10:28.
- phonos (φόνος): Greek murder — Mark 7:21-22; Romans 1:29.
- phoneus (φονεύς): Greek murderer — Acts 7:52; 1 Peter 4:15.
- anthropoktonos (ἀνθρωποκτόνος): Greek "man-killer," used of the adversary in John 8:44 and of the one who hates his brother in 1 John 3:15.

# Additional Information

- The reason for the weight of life goes back to Noah: "Whoever sheds man's blood, by man shall his blood be shed. For in God's own image God made man" (Genesis 9:6). Life is protected because man bears God's image.
- Cain is the first murderer. Abel's blood "cries to me from the ground" (Genesis 4:10), so blood has a voice before Yahweh.
- Bloodshed pollutes the land, and "no atonement can be made for the land for the blood that is shed in it, but by the blood of him who shed it" (Numbers 35:33). A murderer cannot be ransomed (Numbers 35:31).
- Two witnesses are required for a murder conviction (Numbers 35:30; Deuteronomy 17:6).
- The Torah protects the unintentional killer through the cities of refuge, so that innocent blood is not shed among the people (Numbers 35:9-15; Deuteronomy 19:1-10).
- Intent matters in the text. A man who "lies in wait" is a murderer (Exodus 21:14; Numbers 35:20-21), but one who kills "unintentionally" and "without hatred" may flee (Deuteronomy 19:4-6).
- Ahab's seizure of Naboth's vineyard through false witnesses is called ratsach: "Have you killed (ratsach), and also taken possession?" (1 Kings 21:19).
- The prophets list murder among Israel's sins: "swearing, lying, murdering (ratsoach), stealing, and committing adultery" (Hosea 4:2; Jeremiah 7:9). Isaiah says "murderers" now fill the once-faithful city (Isaiah 1:21).
- Yeshua takes the command to the heart. Anger and contempt toward a brother put a person in danger of judgment (Matthew 5:21-22). He traces murder to the heart, "out of the heart come evil thoughts, murders, adultery..." (Matthew 15:19).
