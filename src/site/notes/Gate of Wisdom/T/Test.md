# Define

- Hebrew: nasah (נָסָה) — to test, to try, to prove; in Hebrew mindset _nasah_ carries the sense of putting something under pressure to reveal what it is made of; it is not inherently negative — Yahweh _nasah_ Abraham (Gen. 22:1) and Israel in the wilderness (Deut. 8:2) to reveal and develop faith; but when directed toward Yahweh the dynamic reverses — to test Yahweh is to demand that He prove Himself, to place conditions on trust, to withhold faith until He performs; this is the violation named in Deuteronomy 6:16
- Greek (LXX): ekpeirazo (ἐκπειράζω) — to put to the test, to tempt thoroughly; the compound _ek_ (out of, fully) + _peirazo_ (to test, to try) intensifies the action — a full, deliberate testing; this is the word Yeshua quotes back to Satan in Matthew 4:7 and Luke 4:12; the LXX and NT use _ekpeirazo_ specifically for the illegitimate testing of Yahweh, distinguishing it from the more neutral _peirazo_ (πειράζω)

# Related Words

- _peirazo_ (πειράζω) — to test, to tempt; the broader NT term covering both legitimate testing and temptation toward sin; _ekpeirazo_ is the stronger, more specific form used for testing Yahweh
- _bachan_ (בָּחַן) — to examine, to test, to assay as metal is tested; used of Yahweh testing hearts (Ps. 17:3; Jer. 17:10); more the sense of refining examination than pressure-testing
- _tsarar_ (צָרַר) — to press, to bind, to distress; related to the conditions that lead Israel to test Yahweh — the pressure of circumstances that tempts them to demand proof of His presence
- _massah_ (מַסָּה) — the place name derived from _nasah_ — the place of testing; Exodus 17:7; the event becomes a permanent geographical and theological memorial to Israel's testing of Yahweh

# Additional Information

- The prohibition in Deuteronomy 6:16 is anchored to a specific historical event — _"as you tested him at Massah"_ — making this not an abstract principle but a command rooted in remembered failure
- At Massah (Ex. 17:1-7) Israel's testing took the form of the question _"Is Yahweh among us or not?"_ — demanding proof of presence rather than trusting what had already been demonstrated; this is the essence of what _nasah_ directed toward Yahweh means
- Deuteronomy 8:2 reveals the other side — _"Yahweh your God led you forty years in the wilderness to humble you, to test (_nasah_) you, to know what was in your heart"_ — Yahweh's _nasah_ of Israel is revelatory and formative; Israel's _nasah_ of Yahweh is presumptuous and faithless
- The asymmetry is critical — Yahweh may test man; man may not test Yahweh

# Connections

- Genesis 22:1 — Yahweh _nasah_ Abraham — the legitimate, formative use of testing
- Exodus 17:1-7 — Massah; the event Deuteronomy 6:16 references directly
- Deuteronomy 8:2 — Yahweh tests Israel in the wilderness to reveal what is in the heart
- Psalm 95:8-9 — _"Do not harden your hearts as at Meribah, as in the day of Massah in the wilderness where your fathers tested me"_ — the event canonized as a warning
- Matthew 4:7; Luke 4:12 — Yeshua quotes Deuteronomy 6:16 directly using _ekpeirazo_ — refusing to place conditions on Yahweh's protection even under extreme pressure
- 1 Corinthians 10:9 — _"Neither let us test the Lord as some of them tested him and were destroyed by serpents"_ — the Massah pattern applied to the assembly as a warning
