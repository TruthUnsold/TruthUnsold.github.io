Penalty — Genesis 2:17; Exodus 21:22; Deuteronomy 25:2-3; Romans 6:23; 2 Thessalonians 1:9; 2 Corinthians 2:6

# Define

- Hebrew: onesh (עֹנֶשׁ) / anash (עָנַשׁ) — a fine or penalty imposed by a judge or the injured party. In Exodus 21:22 the husband imposes the fine and the judges set the amount. In Proverbs 17:26; 19:19; 21:11 it is the price the guilty must pay. Hebrew mindset: a penalty is a fixed cost attached to the offense, measured and limited (Deuteronomy 25:2-3).
- Hebrew: mishpat (מִשְׁפָּט) — the judicial verdict that fixes the penalty (Exodus 21:1; Deuteronomy 25:1-2).
- Hebrew: mavet (מָוֶת) — "you shall surely die" (Genesis 2:17), the stated penalty, in the form mot tamut (מוֹת תָּמוּת), literally "dying you shall die."
- Greek (LXX): zemia (ζημία) — the LXX rendering for the fine in Proverbs 17:26 and 19:19. It means loss or damage.
- Greek: dike (δίκη) — penalty or justice. "They will pay the penalty" (2 Thessalonians 1:9; Jude 1:7).
- Greek: epitimia (ἐπιτιμία) — a penalty imposed by the majority of the assembly (2 Corinthians 2:6).
- Greek: opsonia (ὀψώνια) — wages, the payment due for sin: "the wages of sin is death" (Romans 6:23, WEB).

# Related Words

- Ransom: kopher (כֹּפֶר) — Exodus 21:30; Lxx lutron (λύτρον); NT lutron (λύτρον) — Mark 10:45
- Restitution: shalam (שָׁלַם) — Exodus 22:1-9
- Guilt or guilt offering: asham (אָשָׁם) — Leviticus 5:15-16
- Stripes: makkah (מַכָּה) — Deuteronomy 25:3

# Additional Information

- The penalty is capped: "forty stripes" and no more, "lest your brother be degraded" (Deuteronomy 25:3).
- The penalty fits the offense: Exodus 21:23-25; Deuteronomy 19:21.
- Penalty can be substituted by ransom in some cases (Exodus 21:30) but not for murder (Numbers 35:31).
- Death is the stated penalty for sin: Genesis 2:17; Ezekiel 18:4; Romans 6:23.

# Additional Info
- Punishment, in the context of the Bible, refers to the divine consequences or judgments imposed by YAH (God) upon individuals, nations, or humanity for disobedience to His commands, laws, or covenant. It is an expression of God’s justice, holiness, and righteousness, often intended to correct, discipline, or bring about repentance, though it can also serve as a final judgment for unrepentant sin. Punishment may manifest as physical, spiritual, or eternal consequences, such as affliction, exile, death, or separation from God’s presence.
