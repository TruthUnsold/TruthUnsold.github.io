- **Hebrew word**: Shalosh (שָׁלוֹשׁ)
- **Meaning**: Three
- **Represents**: Completeness, divine perfection, harmony
	- Three days typically functions repeatedly as a period marking transition — from danger to safety, from death to life, from one status to another (crossing into the land, approaching a king at risk of death, rising from the tomb). It is consistently a "waiting span" that precedes a decisive deliverance or turning point
- **Paleo-Hebrew symbol**: Resembles a hook or foot (gimel)
- **Hebrew character Representing the number**: ג (gimel)
- **Significant verses in the Bible**:
  -   Jonah 1:17: “Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.”
  - Matthew 12:40 "For as Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the huge fish, so will the Son of Man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth."
  - Abraham "lifted up his eyes, and saw the place [Moriah] far off" on "the third day" of his journey to sacrifice Isaac (Genesis 22:4, WEB)
- Joseph imprisons his brothers "three days" before testing them (Genesis 42:17-18)
- Israel travels "three days' journey" into the wilderness before the first water crisis at Marah (Exodus 15:22)
- Spies hide "three days" from pursuers before returning to Joshua (Joshua 2:16, 22)
- Israel waits "three days" before crossing the Jordan into the land (Joshua 1:11; 3:2)
- Esther fasts "three days, night or day" before approaching the king (Esther 4:16)
- Yeshua is in the tomb and rises on "the third day" (Matthew 16:21; Luke 24:7, 46; 1 Corinthians 15:4)
