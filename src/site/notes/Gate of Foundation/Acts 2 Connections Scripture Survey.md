- [Leviticus 23:15-22]() ([[Gate of Wisdom/Appointed Time/Pentecost\|Pentecost]] / <mark style="background: #A284CDA6;">Shavuot</mark>)
	- [[Gate of Wisdom/Offering/Offerings\|Offerings]] made by fire ([[Gate of Wisdom/Offering/Burnt Offering\|Burnt Offering]])
	- [[Gate of Wisdom/Appointed Time/Holy Convocation\|Holy Convocation]] 
		- The word used is _miqra_ (מִקְרָא), which means "a summoned assembly" or "a called-out gathering". It comes from the root _qara_, meaning "to call" or "to summon"
		- _Ekklesia_ literally means **"the called-out ones"** or a summoned assembly
		- The phrase "together in one place" (_homou epi to auto_) emphasizes a unified corporate gathering. They were in Jerusalem specifically because Leviticus 23:21 legally mandated that exact day to be a holy convocation—a day where you stop regular work and gather together
	- Two loaves
		- In the Levitical sacrificial system, leaven (yeast) was strictly forbidden on the altar because it symbolically represented corruption, sin, or pride.
		- The Pentecost offering is the only instance where God explicitly commands bread to be "baked with leaven" and brought to the sanctuary.
		- In the immediate Hebrew mindset of the first century, the two loaves represented the [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/House of Judah\|House of Judah]] (the Southern Kingdom) and the [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/House of Israel\|House of Israel]] (the Northern Kingdom, which had been scattered among the nations //[[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/Gentile\|Gentile]]s//)
		- From "every nation under heaven"—By uniting them under one Spirit, God was bringing the "two loaves" together as a single offering.
		- Early Christians and later theologians recognized the two loaves as the joining of Jews and Gentiles into "one new man," reconciling both groups to God in one body.
