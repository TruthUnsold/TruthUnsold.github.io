## Name

The word used for name is [[Gate of Wisdom/S/Shem\|Shem]], שֵׁם (pronounced “shem”). It does not refer to what we typically consider a name such as John or Jane. It refers to the character, reputation, authority, or essence of who someone is. All through out the Bible we see “[[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Divine Council/God\|God]]” refer to doing things because of “My [[Gate of Wisdom/N/Name\|Name]].”

- Ezekiel 20:9: “But I acted for the sake of My name, …”
- Ezekiel 36:22-23 “for the sake of My [[Gate of Wisdom/H/Holy\|Holy]] name, …”
- Psalm 23:3: “He restores my soul. He guides me along the right paths for His name’s sake.”

In Greek, the word for “[[Gate of Wisdom/N/Name\|Name]]” is ὄνομα (onoma). It also can refer to character, reputation, authority, or essence of who someone is.

- Matthew 6:9: “Pray then like this: ‘Our Father in [[Gate of Wisdom/Dwelling Places/Realm of Yah/Heaven\|Heaven]], hallowed be your name.'”
- Philippians 2:9: “Therefore God has highly exalted him and bestowed on him the name that is above every name.”
- John 1:12: “But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Children of God\|Children of God]].”

None of these text are referring to a Name such as John or Jane, they are all referring to who “God” is, as a being, his nature. In the Jewish religion, “God” is referred to simply as “Ha-Shem,” “The Name.” They believe the god of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob’s real name is too sacred to utter.

## God

God is not a name – like John or Jane. When we read the word “God,” it is either El, or [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Divine Council/Elohim\|Elohim]], אֱלֹהִים (Elohim). El is singular, Elohim is plural. God is a type of spiritual being, it is not capitalized. θεός (Theos) is the equivalent in Greek meaning “God” or “divine.” There are several religions who could say they follow “god,” but it doesn’t mean the god of [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/Abraham\|Abraham]], [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/Isaac\|Isaac]], and [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/Jacob\|Jacob]]. When we randomly say I am a follower of God – it is like saying, I live with Human. It tells us nothing about who you live with – there are a lot of people who live with a human.

In the ancient Hebrew texts we are reading, god typically refers to the god of the [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/Israelites\|Israelites]]. But there are other ancient text using the word El or Elohim referring to a deity other than the god of the Israelites.

Most people are familiar with El Shaddai – “God Almighty.” Others less familiar are El Elyon – “God Most High,” in Genesis 14:18-22. And El Roi – “God Who Sees Me” or “God of Seeing,” in Genesis 16:13. In each of these, it is not referring to “God” as the name, it is referring to who he is, he is the god most high, the god who sees, and the god almighty. God is a generic term describing a type of being. Its the context of our surround scriptures which lets us know it is the god of the Israelites. The god we are choosing to follow.

We also see ‘el’ added to the end of what we would consider a name, such as ‘Bethel,’ Michael,’ ‘Raphael’. But to an ancient Hebrew they are seeing ‘House of God,’ “Who is like God,’ and ‘God has healed.’ [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/Israel\|Israel]] is ‘Struggles with God.’ These are things we miss in our English translations. There is way more being told to us than simply the name of an “[[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Heavenly Beings/Angel\|Angel]],” or a nation. Because our Bible is all about the god of the Israelites, each of these words is pointing directly back to him by using the word el in what we see as a name.

He does reveal his shem in scripture in Exodus 3:14-15. In English, this would be translated as “I Am Who I Am,” or “I Will Be Who I Will Be.” In Hebrew it would be *אֶהְיֶה אֲשֶׁר אֶהְיֶה (Ehyeh Asher Ehyeh)* in verse 14. In 15: יְהוָה (YHWH). In our English translations this is often represented by LORD. Because of the [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/Jew\|Jew]]ish tradition of using Hashem, how YHWH is pronounced has been lost. There are several guesses on how to pronounce YWHW, but we know for sure his name is not God, El, or Elohim.

In Psalm 68:4 (Psalm 68:5 in some translations) we see a reference to a shortened version of his name, “By His name [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Divine Council/Yah\|Yah]] **יָהּ** ” Here is another reference which uses this name, הַלְלוּיָהּ. I can almost guarantee if you have been a Christian for any length of time, you’ve used this one. This could be translated in our English Bibles as “Praise the LORD,” but typically it is written as Hallelujah … Hallelu-Yah. If you read it as Hallelujah – it is literally an untranslated word, using English letters.

## Lucifer

[[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Heavenly Beings/Lucifer\|Lucifer]] is another instance where we see an untranslated word. It is ***הֵילֵל*** *(Helel)* in Hebrew but the Latin equivalent is Lucifer. When it is translated, we often see “[[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Heavenly Beings/Morning Star\|Morning Star]]” or “[[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Shining One\|Shining One]].” ([[Gate of Wisdom/Sources/Heiser, Michael S/The Unseen Realm\|The Unseen Realm]]) In Isaiah 14:12, some translations will use morning star or shining one, and some opt for Lucifer – with a capital L, as if it is a name. It is not the name of a being.

This word used here in Isaiah is referring to a specific type or phase of a star – it is the morning star. The bright morning star is often referred to as Venus. In Revelation 22:16, Jesus refers to himself as the bright morning star.

The Greek word used in Revelation 12 for star is ἄστρα (astra), it is a more generic term. It is more closely related to the Hebrew word used for “stars” in Genesis 15:5, which is כּוֹכָבִים (kokhavim). Both of these words are used for literal stars in the sky, as well as metaphorically to represent heavenly beings. They are also used to symbolize powerful, divine entities or to express heavenly realms and cosmic events. The context they are used in determines the meaning.

However, Helel or Lucifer, is not a name of a being.

Isaiah 14, a “taunt against the king of [[Gate of Wisdom/Dwelling Places/Realm of Humans/Babylon\|Babylon]],” has become connected to Ezekiel 28. Ezekiel 28 is about “the ruler of Tyre.” These have then been combined with Revelation 12 to ultimately be associated with “the fall of Satan.”

## Satan

In Revelation 12:3-4; 7-9 we read:

Then another sign appeared in heaven: a great fiery red [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Heavenly Beings/Dragon\|Dragon]] that had seven heads and ten horns, and seven royal crowns on his heads. <sup>[<a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=rev%2012&amp;version=TLV#fen-TLV-30956c">c</a>]</sup> <sup>4</sup> His tail sweeps away a third of the stars of heaven—it hurled them to the earth … 7 And war broke out in heaven, Michael and his angels making war against the dragon.\[f\] The dragon and his angels fought, 8 but they were not strong enough, and there was no longer any place for them in heaven. 9 And the great dragon was thrown down—the ancient [[Gate of Wisdom/S/Serpent\|Serpent]], called the devil and satan, who deceives the whole world. (TLV)

Let’s breakdown the “beings” listed here:

- Stars – ἄστρα (astra) discussed previously.
- Michael – *Μιχαήλ* (Mikháel) – Hebrew equivalent is “mikha’el,” which means “Who is like God?”
- Angels – *ἄγγελοι* (ángeloi) – Hebrew equivalent is “mal’akhim” which means [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Messenger\|Messenger]] – it can refer to both human and divine beings. Usually the divine being is bringing an important message, protecting people, or carrying out God’s will. Angels are described as being radiant and shining beings.
- Dragon – δράκων (drákon) – Hebrew word typically connected to this Greek word is Tannin. This word is usually translated in the Hebrew Scriptures as serpent, dragon, or sea monster. It carries the meaning of [[Gate of Wisdom/Consequence/Disobedience/Chaos\|Chaos]], a force of evil. It’s a threat to the divine order of YHWH/God.
- Serpent – *ὄφις* (óphis) – Hebrew connection is Nahas [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Heavenly Beings/Nachash\|Nachash]]), we see this used in Genesis 3:1. This word can be translated as Serpent, Deceiver, or Shining One.
- [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Devil\|Devil]] – *διάβολος* (diábolos) – Hebrew equivalent is Satan meaning [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Adversary\|Adversary]], accuser, or opponent.
- Satan – *Σατανᾶς* (Satanás) – Hebrew equivalent is Satan meaning adversary, accuser, or opponent.

A ‘[[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Satan\|Satan]]’ is not always contrary to the will of YHWH/God, the one in Job is working as a prosecutor in Yah’s court. The one in Numbers 22:22 where it is translated as “oppose” is the [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Heavenly Beings/Angel of the Lord\|Angel of the Lord]] standing in the way. But in 1 Chronicles 21 the being enticed David as an adversary to Yah’s will.

Who this being is in Revelation 12 is for you to determine. Is it connected to [[Gate of Wisdom/Beings/Human Beings/Isaiah\|Isaiah]] and Ezekiel? Is it the one ultimate arch nemesis to YHWH/God? Is it a leader of a rebellion in the heavenly realm?

Whatever you determine this being to be, we can definitively state it is not a being named “Satan.”

“God,” “Lucifer,” and “Satan” are not names like John and Jane, they are names like the Hebrew word Shem – they are describing the character, reputation, authority, or essence of a being.
