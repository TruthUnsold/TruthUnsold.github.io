List of Commands Israel Broke

No Other Gods Before Yahweh

Exodus 20:3; Deuteronomy 5:7
"You shall have no other gods before me."

  • Broken:

    • Israel served the Baals after Joshua's death — Judges 2:11–12
    • Served Baal-Berith after Gideon's death — Judges 8:33
    • Served gods of surrounding nations — Judges 10:6
    • Solomon's heart turned after other gods — 1 Kings 11:4–8
    • Jeroboam's golden calves at Bethel and Dan — 1 Kings 12:28–30
    • Ahab served Baal and Asherah (Ishtar/Easter) — 1 Kings 16:30–33
    • Northern kingdom's sustained idolatry — 2 Kings 17:7–8
    • Idolatry within the temple itself — Ezekiel 8:10–16
    • Burned incense to the Baals — Hosea 2:13

  • Prescribed Remedy:

    • Death for the idolater — Deuteronomy 17:2–5;
    • no offering covers deliberate high-handed idolatry — Numbers 15:30–31;
    • repentance and return to the covenant — Deuteronomy 30:1–3

  • What Actually Happened:

    • Three thousand men killed by the sword after the golden calf — Exodus 32:27–28
    • A plague sent by Yahweh upon Israel — Exodus 32:35
    • Cycles of oppression and foreign domination throughout Judges — Judges 2:14–15
    • Solomon's kingdom torn from his son — 1 Kings 11:11–13
    • Northern kingdom carried into Assyrian exile — 2 Kings 17:18–23
    • Southern kingdom carried into Babylonian exile — 2 Kings 24:3–4

No Carved Images or Idols

Exodus 20:4–5; Deuteronomy 5:8–9
"You shall not make for yourself a carved image, or any likeness of anything that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth. You shall not bow yourself down to them, nor serve them."

  • Broken:

    • Aaron fashioned the golden calf — Exodus 32:4
    • Micah made a carved image and household idols — Judges 17:3–5
    • Tribe of Dan stole Micah's carved image and set it up — Judges 18:14–20
    • Jeroboam's two golden calves — 1 Kings 12:28
    • Carved images on the walls of the temple — Ezekiel 8:10
    • Making molten images of silver — Hosea 13:2
    • Israel making idols condemned — Isaiah 44:9–17

  • Prescribed Remedy:

    • Death — Deuteronomy 17:2–5;
    • destruction of the idol required — Deuteronomy 7:5;
    • no offering covers deliberate idol worship — Numbers 15:30–31

  • What Actually Happened:

    • Three thousand killed after the golden calf — Exodus 32:27–28
    • A plague upon Israel — Exodus 32:35
    • Dan's idolatry at the carved image endured throughout the period of the tabernacle at Shiloh — Judges 18:31
    • Jeroboam's sin became the defining charge against every subsequent northern king — 1 Kings 16:26
    • Northern kingdom exiled to Assyria — 2 Kings 17:22–23

Do Not Make Molten Gods

Exodus 34:17; Leviticus 19:4
"You shall make no molten gods for yourselves."

  • Broken:

    • Aaron fashioned the golden calf — Exodus 32:4
    • Jeroboam's two golden calves set up at Bethel and Dan — 1 Kings 12:28
    • Making molten images of silver — Hosea 13:2
    • Micah's mother making a carved and molten image — Judges 17:4

  • Prescribed Remedy: Cherem (חֵרֶם) — devoted to destruction — Exodus 22:20; death — Deuteronomy 17:2–5; no offering covers deliberate idol-making — Numbers 15:30–31; Moses' intercession as the only path when no offering suffices — Exodus 32:30–32

  • What Actually Happened:

    • Three thousand killed by the sword — Exodus 32:27–28
    • A plague sent upon Israel — Exodus 32:35
    • Jeroboam's sin became the defining indictment repeated against every northern king — 1 Kings 16:26; 2 Kings 17:22–23
    • Northern kingdom exiled to Assyria — [2 Kings 17:18](

Do Not Take the Name of YHWH in Vain

Exodus 20:7; Deuteronomy 5:11
"You shall not take the name of Yahweh your God in vain."

  • Broken:

    • Son of an Israelite woman blasphemed the Name — Leviticus 24:11
    • Israel profaned Yahweh's name among the nations through their conduct — Ezekiel 36:20–22
    • Priests despised Yahweh's name with polluted offerings — Malachi 1:6–7
    • Profaned Yahweh's name by re-enslaving freed servants — Jeremiah 34:16
    • Profaned the holy name through sexual immorality — Amos 2:7

  • Prescribed Remedy:

    • Death for blasphemy of the Name — Leviticus 24:16;
    • the blasphemer brought outside the camp and stoned — Leviticus 24:14

  • What Actually Happened:

    • The blasphemer was stoned as prescribed — Leviticus 24:23
    • Yahweh's name continually profaned among the nations as a consequence of Israel's exile and conduct — Ezekiel 36:20
    • Priests rebuked and the priesthood threatened with removal — Malachi 2:1–3

Remember the Sabbath and Keep It Holy

Exodus 20:8; Deuteronomy 5:12; Exodus 31:13
"Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy."

  • Broken:

    • Man gathering sticks on the Sabbath in the wilderness — Numbers 15:32–33
    • Israel profaned Yahweh's Sabbaths in the wilderness — Ezekiel 20:13
    • The next generation also profaned the Sabbaths — Ezekiel 20:21
    • Treading winepresses and trading on the Sabbath — Nehemiah 13:15–16
    • Carrying burdens through Jerusalem's gates on the Sabbath — Jeremiah 17:21–23
    • Despising holy things and profaning Sabbaths — Ezekiel 22:8
    • Asking when the Sabbath would end to sell grain — Amos 8:5

  • Prescribed Remedy:

    • Death ; cut off from the people — Exodus 31:14–15;
    • no offering covers deliberate Sabbath violation — Numbers 15:30–31

  • What Actually Happened:

    • The man gathering sticks was stoned to death — Numbers 15:35–36
    • Yahweh withheld His hand in the wilderness and threatened destruction — Ezekiel 20:13–14
    • Jerusalem's gates threatened with fire for Sabbath breaking — Jeremiah 17:27
    • The land kept its Sabbaths during seventy years of exile — 2 Chronicles 36:21
    • Nehemiah shut the gates of Jerusalem on the Sabbath and drove out the merchants — Nehemiah 13:19–21

Honor Your Father and Mother

Exodus 20:12; Deuteronomy 5:16
"Honor your father and your mother."

  • Broken:

    • Father and mother treated with contempt in Jerusalem — Ezekiel 22:7
    • Son dishonors father; daughter rises against mother — Micah 7:6
    • Absalom's full rebellion against his father David — 2 Samuel 15:1–12
    • General contempt for parents listed among Jerusalem's sins — Proverbs 30:17

  • Prescribed Remedy:

    • Death for striking a parent — Exodus 21:15;
    • death for cursing a parent — Exodus 21:17; Leviticus 20:9;
    • a stubborn and rebellious son brought before the elders and stoned — Deuteronomy 21:18–21

  • What Actually Happened:

    • Absalom — though not executed by David — was eventually killed by Joab; David mourned deeply — 2 Samuel 18:14–15; 2 Samuel 18:33
    • Jerusalem's contempt for parents listed as part of the indictment leading to exile — Ezekiel 22:15

Do Not Murder

Exodus 20:13; Deuteronomy 5:17
"You shall not murder."

  • Broken:

    • David arranged Uriah's death — 2 Samuel 11:14–17
    • Jezebel's conspiracy to murder Naboth — 1 Kings 21:9–14
    • Manasseh filled Jerusalem with innocent blood — 2 Kings 21:16
    • Murder listed among Jerusalem's sins — Ezekiel 22:3–6
    • Killing among Israel's listed transgressions — Hosea 4:2
    • Sons and daughters sacrificed — blood touching blood — Psalm 106:37–38

  • Prescribed Remedy:

    • Death — the murderer shall surely be put to death — Exodus 21:12; Numbers 35:16–21;
    • no ransom accepted for a murderer's life — Numbers 35:31;
    • blood defiles the land and only the blood of the murderer cleanses it — Numbers 35:33

  • What Actually Happened:

    • David's household struck with perpetual sword; Uriah's blood never formally avenged — 2 Samuel 12:10–12
    • Ahab and Jezebel — dogs licked Ahab's blood; Jezebel eaten by dogs on the plot of ground that was Naboth's — 1 Kings 22:38; 2 Kings 9:36–37
    • Manasseh's bloodshed cited as primary cause of Judah's exile — Yahweh would not pardon — 2 Kings 24:3–4
    • Jerusalem destroyed; the city's blood guilt a central charge — Ezekiel 24:6–9

Do Not Commit Adultery

Exodus 20:14; Deuteronomy 5:18
"You shall not commit adultery."

  • Broken:

    • David and Bathsheba — 2 Samuel 11:4
    • Widespread adultery among the people — Jeremiah 5:7–8
    • Men committing adultery with neighbors' wives in Jerusalem — Ezekiel 22:11
    • Adultery among Israel's transgressions — Hosea 4:2
    • Daughters committing harlotry; men going apart with harlots — Hosea 4:13–14
    • Treachery against the wife of youth — Malachi 2:14–15

  • Prescribed Remedy:

    • Death for both parties — Leviticus 20:10; Deuteronomy 22:22;
    • guilt offering required in the specific case involving a betrothed slave woman — Leviticus 19:20–22;
    • no offering covers deliberate adultery — Numbers 15:30–31

  • What Actually Happened:

    • David's child by Bathsheba died; the sword remained in David's house; his wives given to another before his eyes — 2 Samuel 12:11–18
    • Israel treated as an adulterous wife — sent away with a certificate of divorce — Jeremiah 3:8
    • Judah's adultery judged through the imagery of Ezekiel 16 — Jerusalem given into the hands of her lovers and stripped bare — Ezekiel 16:37–41

Do Not Steal

Exodus 20:15; Deuteronomy 5:19
"You shall not steal."

  • Broken:

    • Achan stole devoted things from Jericho — Joshua 7:20–21
    • Making the ephah small and the shekel great — theft through commerce — Amos 8:5
    • Seizing fields and houses by violence — Micah 2:1–2
    • Stealing listed among Israel's transgressions — Hosea 4:2
    • Robbing Yahweh in tithes and offerings — Malachi 3:8
    • Stealing listed among temple-court sins — Jeremiah 7:9

  • Prescribed Remedy:

    • Restitution Offering — fivefold for an ox; fourfold for a sheep — Exodus 22:1;
    • double restitution if the animal found alive — Exodus 22:4;
    • restitution plus one-fifth with a Guilt Offering (asham, אָשָׁם) when theft involved a false oath — Leviticus 6:5–7

  • What Actually Happened:

    • Achan, his family, and all he had were stoned and burned — Joshua 7:24–26
    • Israel defeated at Ai — thirty-six men killed — Joshua 7:4–5
    • Agricultural theft and commercial dishonesty listed in Yahweh's indictment leading to exile — Amos 8:7–8
    • Curse upon the whole nation for robbing Yahweh in tithes — Malachi 3:9

Do Not Bear False Witness

Exodus 20:16; Deuteronomy 5:20
"You shall not give false testimony against your neighbor."

  • Broken:

    • False witnesses brought against Naboth — 1 Kings 21:10–13
    • Swearing falsely while swearing by Yahweh's name — Jeremiah 5:2
    • Men carrying slanders to shed blood — Ezekiel 22:9
    • Lying among Israel's transgressions — Hosea 4:2

The Weight of Israel's Rebellion

  • Yahweh's anger was kindled against Israel and He sold them into the hands of their enemies — Plunder, oppression, and military defeat were the direct consequences of covenant breaking
  • The nation was handed over to foreign powers in cycles — each period of oppression in the book of Judges directly tied to specific acts of covenant unfaithfulness
  • The northern kingdom of Israel was carried into Assyrian exile and did not return — the land was repopulated with foreign peoples; ten tribes effectively lost to covenant community
  • The southern kingdom of Judah was carried into Babylonian exile for seventy years — corresponding precisely to the number of Sabbath years the land had not rested
  • The land itself vomited out its inhabitants — the land's exile was Yahweh keeping His own Sabbaths that Israel had denied it
  • The temple was destroyed — first by Nebuchadnezzar in 586 BC; the very house of Yahweh was burned because those who served in it had profaned it
  • The ark of the covenant was captured by the Philistines — Israel's central covenant object removed from them as a consequence of presumptuous battle without Yahweh's blessing
  • Famine, drought, and agricultural failure were sent — Yahweh closed the heavens over a land that withheld firstfruits, robbed tithes, and broke Sabbath years
  • Sword, pestilence, and plague came upon the people — the covenant curses of Deuteronomy 28 executed in full sequence
  • The priesthood became corrupt and was judged — Eli's house cut off; the sons of Eli killed on the same day; later, priests who offered polluted sacrifices shamed before all the people
  • Kings who turned to other gods saw their dynasties cut off — Saul's kingdom torn from him; Solomon's kingdom divided; Jeroboam's line annihilated
  • Child sacrifice to Molech and demons required blood atonement that no individual offering could cover — this was a high-handed sin bringing national judgment
  • No single sin offering covered the nation's intentional, repeated, high-handed covenant violations — only national repentance, return, and Yahweh's own mercy could restore what was broken
  • Yahweh's mercy remained open — even in exile, return and restoration were promised upon genuine repentance and turning back to the covenant with the whole heart
