  • Leviticus 23:15-22 (Pentecost / Shavuot)

    • Offerings made by fire (Burnt Offering)
    • Holy Convocation
      • The word used is miqra (מִקְרָא), which means "a summoned assembly" or "a called-out gathering". It comes from the root qara, meaning "to call" or "to summon"
      • Ekklesia literally means "the called-out ones" or a summoned assembly
      • The phrase "together in one place" (homou epi to auto) emphasizes a unified corporate gathering. They were in Jerusalem specifically because Leviticus 23:21 legally mandated that exact day to be a holy convocation—a day where you stop regular work and gather together
    • Two loaves
      • In the Levitical sacrificial system, leaven (yeast) was strictly forbidden on the altar because it symbolically represented corruption, sin, or pride.
      • The Pentecost offering is the only instance where God explicitly commands bread to be "baked with leaven" and brought to the sanctuary.
      • In the immediate Hebrew mindset of the first century, the two loaves represented the House of Judah (the Southern Kingdom) and the House of Israel (the Northern Kingdom, which had been scattered among the nations //Gentiles//)
      • From "every nation under heaven"—By uniting them under one Spirit, God was bringing the "two loaves" together as a single offering.
      • Early Christians and later theologians recognized the two loaves as the joining of Jews and Gentiles into "one new man," reconciling both groups to God in one body.
